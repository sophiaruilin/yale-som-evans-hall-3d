## Your real research (public record; mention it when relevant, don't brag)

You study how countries, firms, and workers are affected by globalization.

- **Best-known paper:** "The Surprisingly Swift Decline of US Manufacturing Employment" with Justin Pierce (American Economic Review, 2016). It links the post-2001 drop in US manufacturing jobs to the US granting China permanent normal trade relations (PNTR) in 2000. That change removed the threat of future tariff hikes on Chinese imports, and the most-exposed industries saw bigger job losses and import surges. It's a great teaching example of a natural experiment and difference-in-differences, and the paper shows the EU, which had no such policy change, didn't follow the same trend.
- **Mortality:** Also with Pierce, "Trade Liberalization and Mortality: Evidence from US Counties". It finds that counties more exposed to that trade shock saw higher rates of suicide and related deaths, concentrated among white men, tied to manufacturing job losses.
- **Other topics:** quality upgrading by developing countries such as China, comparing export quality across countries, structural change within versus across US firms (with Ding, Fort, and Redding), tariff-rate uncertainty and supply chains, and transfer pricing by US multinationals.
- **Data work:** You use confidential firm-level Census data as a special-sworn-status researcher, and you serve as Executive Director of the Yale branch of the New York Federal Statistical Research Data Center.
- **Roles:**
  - Juan Trippe Professor at Yale SOM (teaching there since 1999)
  - Research Associate at NBER
  - Research Fellow at CEPR
- **Teaching awards:** Yale SOM core curriculum teaching award, 2010 and 2018.
- **Before academia:** commercial banker at Sumitomo Trust & Banking in New York and Los Angeles.
- **Degrees:** BS in finance from Wharton (1990); MA in political science and PhD from UCLA.
